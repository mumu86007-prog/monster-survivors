Chess Strategy for Beginners: 10 Principles to Win Your First Games

By omggame.store editorial team · 8 min read

Chess has a reputation for being intimidating, but the fundamentals are surprisingly simple. You don't need to memorize 20 moves of opening theory to start winning games. You just need to understand 10 core principles that govern every phase of the game. This guide will take you from "I know how the pieces move" to "I have a plan."

Before We Start: Every piece has a point value: Pawn=1, Knight=3, Bishop=3, Rook=5, Queen=9. The King is priceless. If you're up 3+ points of material, trade pieces aggressively — you'll win the endgame.

The 10 Principles

Opening (Moves 1-10)

  1. Control the center. Your first moves should place pawns or pieces on or toward the four central squares (d4, d5, e4, e5). Center control gives your pieces maximum mobility.
  2. Develop knights before bishops. Knights belong on f3/c3 (or f6/c6 for Black). They're short-range pieces that need to be in the action early. Bishops can influence the center from their starting squares.
  3. Don't move the same piece twice. Each move in the opening should develop a new piece. Moving the same piece twice while your other pieces sit at home is the #1 beginner mistake.
  4. Castle before move 10. Kings belong behind a wall of pawns. Delaying castling leaves your king exposed in the center where it's vulnerable to attacks.

Middlegame (Moves 10-30)

  1. Connect your rooks. After castling and developing minor pieces, your rooks should "see" each other with no pieces between them. Connected rooks control open files and support each other.
  2. Look for checks, captures, and threats — every move. Before you move, ask: Can I check the enemy king? Can I capture anything for free? Can I threaten something valuable? Then ask the same from your opponent's perspective.
  3. Don't hang pieces. "Hanging" means leaving a piece unprotected where it can be captured. Before moving, verify your destination square is safe. This single habit will improve your rating by 300 points.

Endgame (Moves 30+)

  1. Activate your king. In the endgame, the king transforms from a liability to a weapon. March it to the center — it's worth about 4 points of attacking power.
  2. Push passed pawns. A passed pawn (no enemy pawns blocking its path to promotion) is a game-winning asset. Push it relentlessly. The opponent must dedicate pieces to stopping it.
  3. Learn the basic checkmates. King+Queen vs King and King+Rook vs King are patterns you must know cold. Practice them until they're automatic — there's nothing more frustrating than reaching a won endgame and not knowing how to finish.

The Scholar's Mate (And How to Defend It)

Many beginners try the 4-move checkmate: 1.e4 e5 2.Qh5 Nc6 3.Bc4 Nf6?? 4.Qxf7#. It preys on beginners who don't see the threat. The defense is simple: after 2.Qh5, play 2...Nc6 (protecting e5). After 3.Bc4, play 3...g6, attacking the queen and blocking the mate. The opponent just wasted two moves and now has an exposed queen. You're already ahead.
