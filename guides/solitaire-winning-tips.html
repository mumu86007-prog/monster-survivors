How to Win at Solitaire: 12 Pro Strategies for Klondike

By omggame.store editorial team · 5 min read

Classic Klondike Solitaire has a win rate of roughly 30-50% depending on the deal. But skilled players consistently win 70%+ by applying strategic thinking rather than making moves at random. Here are 12 strategies that will dramatically improve your game.

Stat Fact: Not every deal is winnable. Approximately 20-30% of Klondike deals are impossible regardless of skill. Don't get discouraged by unwinnable games — focus on winning the ones that are possible.

  1. Draw from the stock before making tableau moves. Every turn, your first action should be drawing from the stock pile. New cards unlock options that weren't available before. Only after drawing should you evaluate tableau moves.
  2. Prioritize revealing face-down cards. Face-down cards are dead resources. Every face-down card revealed doubles the information and options available to you. When choosing between two equal-looking moves, pick the one that reveals a face-down card.
  3. Build foundation piles evenly. Don't rush one suit to completion. If you complete Hearts while Spades is still at 3, you'll struggle to find places for black cards. Keep all four suits within 2-3 cards of each other.
  4. Empty columns are gold. An empty column can only hold a King — but that's incredibly powerful. It gives you a place to move any King that's blocking your progress. Prioritize creating empty columns over almost anything else.
  5. Kings are trouble — manage them early. Kings are the hardest cards to place. They can only go in empty columns. If you have multiple Kings available, move the one that reveals the most face-down cards or unblocks the most tableau.
  6. Don't automatically build on the tableau. Just because you can move a red 7 onto a black 8 doesn't mean you should. Sometimes leaving cards in place preserves more options. Ask: does this move reveal a face-down card? Does it create an empty column?
  7. Prefer moving from columns with more face-down cards. Given identical moves, move from the column with more face-down cards underneath. This gets you closer to revealing them.
  8. Aces and Twos are priority plays. Move these to the foundation immediately. They have almost no strategic value on the tableau and free up space.
  9. Use undo sparingly — it's a learning tool, not a crutch. Undo helps you learn "I shouldn't have made that move." Use it to understand why a move was bad, not to brute-force solutions.
  10. In draw-3 mode, track the discard pile order. Cards recycle in the same order. Knowing what's coming helps you plan.
  11. When stuck, reconsider your tableau layout. Look for alternative arrangements. Sometimes moving a sequence from one column to another opens up possibilities you missed.
  12. Practice makes permanent, not perfect. Only perfect practice makes perfect. Play deliberately — always know WHY you're making each move. Mindless clicking reinforces bad habits.
